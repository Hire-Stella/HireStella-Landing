# Image brief — 20 photographic covers

Premium, realistic, 8K. Every image is built from what its page actually says, so the picture and
the words argue the same thing. Generate with Nano Banana, save into this folder
(`06-Website/public/visuals/`) under the file name given. I convert, size and wire them in.

## Before you start

**Format.** Landscape **3:2**, target **1536 x 1024** (generate at 8K if the model offers it and I
will downscale — a downscaled 8K frame is sharper than a native small one). PNG or JPG.

**The look, in one line.** Blue hour. A real, expensive, modern interior photographed after the
lights come on, where the ambient light is deep midnight navy and the only warmth in the frame is
one practical light. That is what makes twenty photographs sit on a navy website and on a white one
without either theme fighting them.

**Paste this style block at the end of every prompt:**

> Ultra-realistic editorial architectural photography, 8K, shot on a full-frame camera with a 35mm
> prime at f/2.8, ISO 200, tripod, natural perspective with vertical lines kept true. Blue hour:
> deep midnight-navy ambient light through glass, with a single warm amber practical light as the
> only warmth in the frame. Premium contemporary interior design, honed stone, brushed metal,
> walnut, glass. Shallow depth of field, true reflections, fine natural grain. Colour graded with
> deep blue shadows and restrained warm highlights, nothing blown out. Calm, quiet, expensive,
> nobody rushing. Composition centred with generous negative space so it still reads at thumbnail
> size. Landscape 3:2.

**Paste this as the negative prompt on every image:**

> No text, no letters, no numbers, no signage, no logos, no brand marks, no readable screens. No
> faces, no portraits, no people looking at camera, no smiling staff, no stock-photo posing. No
> clutter, no cables, no HDR halos, no heavy vignette, no fisheye or wide-angle distortion, no
> watermark, no illustration or 3D-render look.

**Three rules that hold the set together.**

1. **One warm light per frame.** Everything else is the blue of the hour. If a generation comes back
   with warm light everywhere, it will not sit on the site — regenerate it.
2. **People only as distance.** Where a prompt allows a figure, it is one person, far away, out of
   focus, turned away. Never a face. Most frames have nobody in them at all, and that is the point:
   these are rooms photographed in the quiet minute the work is not being answered.
3. **No readable screens.** A glowing monitor is fine; anything legible on it will come out as
   nonsense text and give the image away.

### `coordination.png`

### `hospitals.png` — **REGENERATE, the first one is not usable**
**Page says:** *One front door. Every department behind it.* A routine request reaches three
departments before one of them owns it.

The frame you generated came back with legible wayfinding signage, an invented **Mercy Health**
logo on the reception wall and a member of staff at the desk. An invented healthcare brand on a
HireStella page implies a customer that does not exist, so it cannot ship. The hospital reception
photograph is standing in on that page meanwhile. This version removes the reception wall, which is
what attracted the signage in the first place:

> A contemporary hospital atrium photographed from just inside the main entrance at blue hour,
> completely empty, with no reception desk and no furniture anywhere in the centre of the frame.
> Behind the entrance the space opens into four separate corridors leading away at different
> angles, each receding into cool blue depth. A single warm amber ceiling fixture hangs high above
> the middle of the atrium, the only warm light in the picture, and its pool of light falls on bare
> polished terrazzo. Every wall is plain stone and glass, entirely blank, with no signage, no
> lettering, no notice boards, no wall panels and nobody in the building.

## When the files land

Drop them in this folder and tell me. I will convert to WebP, check each at 1536 x 1024 against
both the dark and the light theme, and wire them so every industry and every segment page carries
its own photograph instead of the shared one. Then I re-run the layout and mobile audits, because a
cover changes card height.

**One thing this brief cannot fix on its own.** The reading room shows *articles*, and there are
only three of them — two for clinics and one general. Real estate, banking and automotive currently
have nothing of their own to show. If you want two articles per industry so every reading room
carries a proper three-card row, say so and I will write them; the covers above are ready for it
either way.
